Living with "Invisible Symptoms"

— Voices of Myasthenia Gravis Patients

To You, Living with Invisible Symptoms

Myasthenia gravis is a designated intractable disease with many symptoms that are
difficult to see from the outside, and it often takes time to reach a diagnosis.
Symptoms can also fluctuate depending on the time of day and physical condition,
making it difficult for those around you to fully understand. This "invisibility"
can lead to burdens not only in daily life but also emotionally.

We interviewed seven myasthenia gravis patients and compiled their experiences
on the path to diagnosis, challenges in daily life, navigating medical care,
and the thoughts born from their experiences.

The experience from hard-to-notice symptoms to reaching a diagnosis

Path to Diagnosis

Myasthenia gravis has early symptoms that are difficult to recognize, and it can take time to reach a diagnosis. Here, we summarize patients' experiences of initial discomfort, confusion, and uncertainty on the way to diagnosis.

  • I thought it was just fatigue
  • I visited multiple departments without getting a diagnosis
  • Antibodies were not detected, and a psychogenic cause was suspected
  • Learning it was a "designated intractable disease" through an internet search heightened my anxiety

Living with daily fluctuations while finding ways to cope

Daily Life Challenges

Because the symptoms are "invisible," difficulties can arise that are hard for others to understand. Here, we summarize the realities of living with symptom fluctuations, daily coping strategies, and emotional burden.

  • Symptoms worsen in the evening / vary greatly day to day
  • Increased difficulty with walking, stairs, and fine motor tasks
  • The challenge of choosing not to push yourself
  • The emotional toll of not being understood

Treatment choices and the relationship with healthcare providers

Navigating Medical Care

There is no single treatment, and choices must be made to fit one's lifestyle and values. Here, we summarize voices about treatment decisions and relationships with healthcare providers.

  • Choosing based on efficacy, side effects, cost, and impact on daily life
  • Building trust through explanation and ongoing dialogue
  • Organizing information during periods of high anxiety
  • Relationships with primary physicians and nurses

The power of connection, and what we want you to know

Messages to Share

Meeting others with the same disease and having a safe space to talk can be a source of emotional support. Here, we summarize the messages patients want to share.

  • Through social media and communities, I felt "I'm not alone"
  • Finding the right fit with healthcare providers and support contacts matters
  • Knowing about support systems and public resources
  • Don't push yourself — build a support network you can rely on
