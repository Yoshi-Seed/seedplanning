Messages to Share

The Power of Connection

Many patients felt strong isolation and anxiety at the time of diagnosis. They shared that connecting with others who have the same disease through social media helped them feel "I'm not alone." While some felt hesitation or anxiety about direct interaction, spaces where they could share information and feelings while maintaining a comfortable distance served as emotional support for many.

What We Want You to Know

Messages based on personal experience were shared: "Don't give up even if antibodies aren't detected," "Always tell your doctor about any discomfort you feel," and "Find a healthcare facility and someone you can talk to that's right for you." Many felt it is important to know about support systems and public resources, and to not push yourself — instead, lean on the help that's available — for living with the disease long-term.

Patient Voices

At the time of diagnosis, she had no opportunity to connect with others with the same disease and felt isolated. Now, through social media and patient groups, she has found support through these connections. She wants to share the message: connect with others who have the same disease, don't carry the burden alone, and find someone you can rely on.

Patient A (Female, 20s)

He made the decision to live as a person with a disability and has planned his life while informing those around him about relevant systems and support. Because social media contains much misinformation about support systems, he actively responds when asked, driven by a desire to share accurate information. He wants people to find a place to consult rather than bearing everything on their own…

Patient B (Male, 50s)

She feels that symptoms you might dismiss as "just being tired" could actually lead to a diagnosis when communicated to your doctor. Because compatibility with healthcare providers greatly influences the course of treatment, she emphasizes the importance of finding the right doctor and medical facility. Also, connecting with others who share the same disease through social media and having someone to share concerns with has been a great source of support…

Patient C (Female, 30s)

Seeing posts from others with the same disease on social media and feeling "I'm not the only one" has been a source of emotional support. While she had some hesitation about direct interaction, she feels there should be spaces where people can participate comfortably according to their stage. She also wishes she had known in advance that some tests and treatments are actually less burdensome than the "scary information" suggests…

Patient D (Female, 40s)

Although she felt very pessimistic at the time of diagnosis, she has come to realize that "things do settle down eventually." Connecting with others who have the same disease through social media and being able to share information about treatment and surgery has been a tremendous source of emotional support. She wants to tell others: don't carry it alone — build relationships where you can consult with people who share your condition…

Patient E (Female, 30s)

While it took time to accept the disease, she now values not pushing herself, relying on others she can trust, and not doing what she can't do. She feels that resting — even with some guilt — when she exceeds her capacity is important for living with the disease long-term, and she wants to convey that message to others with the same condition.

Patient F (Female, 30s)

Because his period without a diagnosis was long, he wants to convey the importance of seeking other medical institutions and getting multiple opinions. Having been helped by other patients' experiences and hospital information shared on social media, he strongly feels the importance of spaces where people with the same disease can connect.
