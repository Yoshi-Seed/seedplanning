Path to Diagnosis

Initial Signs

Many patients noticed symptoms such as easy fatigue, eye problems (drooping eyelids, double vision), and difficulty exerting strength in their arms and legs. However, quite a few attributed these to aging, general fatigue, work stress, or orthopedic issues, and did not immediately suspect myasthenia gravis.

The Road to Diagnosis

The first departments visited varied — ophthalmology, orthopedics, internal medicine, and others — and many cases involved visits to multiple medical institutions before being referred to neurology. Some patients reported that periods of inconclusive test results, or being told their symptoms might be psychogenic, led to significant anxiety and confusion.

Receiving the Diagnosis

The time to diagnosis varied widely, from a few weeks to over six months or nearly a year. When the diagnosis was finally confirmed, many patients reported feeling both shock and anxiety, along with relief that there was finally an explanation — that their symptoms finally made sense.

Because she already had poor eyesight, she initially thought the cause was contact lens fatigue and visited an ophthalmologist. However, after visiting multiple ophthalmologists without receiving a diagnosis, she went through neurosurgery and internal medicine before a general hospital first raised the possibility of myasthenia gravis…

She came across the term "myasthenia gravis" through an internet search and underwent testing with growing anxiety, but no diagnosis was reached…
